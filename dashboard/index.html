Pre-Print Publication

When Is Binary Serialization Worth the Trade-Off?

An End-to-End Workload- and Network-Aware Empirical Evaluation of JSON, Compressed JSON, and MessagePack Serialization

ACM / IEEE Systems Track 28 Pages 10 Canonical Workloads Break-Even Modeling PDF

Abstract & Key Contributions

In modern distributed systems, web architectures, and microservice APIs, data interchange serialization format is often chosen based on intuition rather than empirical trade-off analysis. While text JSON offers ubiquitous compatibility and MessagePack provides compact binary representations, total end-to-end latency is governed by the coupled dynamics of serialization CPU time (T_ser), deserialization time (T_deser), and simulated physical network transit time (T_net = Payload / Bandwidth). This research introduces a repeatable, zero-bias benchmarking methodology and presents an analytical 3-Zone Decision Framework along with a closed-form break-even bandwidth equation (B_BE). Our empirical evaluations across 10 diverse workload payloads and 3 network bandwidth tiers demonstrate precisely under which operational regimes binary serialization or compression provides statistically significant wall-clock speedups.
